The Trick Revealed

When you first pick a door, there's a 1/3 chance you picked the car and a 2/3 chance the car is behind one of the other two doors. The host, who knows where the car is, always opens a door you didn't pick that has a goat. That doesn't change your original 1/3 — but it does concentrate the other doors' combined 2/3 probability entirely onto the one remaining unopened door. Switching lets you trade your 1/3 door for that 2/3 door.

See it as conditional probability

Let C be the event "car is behind your original door" — P(C) = 1/3. The host's action is not random: he is constrained to avoid both your door and the car. Because the host's choice depends on where the car is, revealing a goat gives you information about the other two doors, not about your own. Formally, P(car is behind the remaining door | host reveals a goat) = 1 − P(C) = 2/3, since the two unchosen doors together always held 2/3 of the probability and revealing a goat door with certainty (given the host's rule) shifts none of that probability back onto your original pick.
